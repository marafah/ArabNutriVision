Seeing beneath the rice: recipe-aware estimation of hidden fat in Arabic composite dishes

Mohammad Arafah1 · Amna Zaytoun2
1Faculty of Information Technology, University of Petra, Amman, Jordan · 2Clinical nutrition, MSc

Food compositionImage-based dietary assessmentVision–language modelsUncertainty

The gap

Food-image systems have learned to name Middle Eastern dishes with high accuracy, but none reports a validated nutrient error for Levantine composite dishes. In these dishes the energy-dense components (frying oil, ghee, tahini, fatty meat) are mixed into or hidden beneath rice and bread. A camera sees the dish; it cannot see how it was cooked. Generic multimodal AI models therefore fail silently: recent evaluations report the largest errors for fat and for complex meals.

The idea

Make the recipe the unit of estimation, and report preparation as an interval rather than hide it in the error:

Ê = m̂ × ed,s / 100    ed,s = 100 · Σk wdsk · rk · ek / Yds
m̂ portion (g) · d dish · s preparation style · w raw ingredient grams · r retention · e ingredient nutrient density · Y cooked yield

Research questions

  1. RQ1. How accurate are multimodal AI models on Arabic composite dishes against weighed ground truth, and is fat the largest error source?
  2. RQ2. Does recipe-aware estimation (dish + style + portion + recipe table) reduce energy and fat error relative to zero-shot image-only estimates?
  3. RQ3. How much of the remaining error is portion, recognition, or preparation? (error-budget decomposition)
  4. RQ4. Do variant-based intervals reach their nominal coverage of true values?
Contributions
  • An Arabic Recipe Composition Table with preparation variants and propagated uncertainty.
  • A quantified "preparation-blind" error floor for any image-only estimator.
  • A recipe-aware pipeline that couples a vision–language model with the table.
  • An open validation protocol (ArabFood-N) and this open-source tool.
